=== filou_pirates
titre: Filou and the Upside-Down Map

Across the bright blue sea speeds The Seashell, the smallest pirate ship in the whole wide world. Right at the front, with his nose in the breeze and his neckerchief flapping, stands Filou.
Filou is a fox who can do anything. Or so he says, puffing out his chest with pride!
“Climbing the mast? Easy! Tying sailor knots? Easy! Chatting with seagulls? Ea-sy-peasy!”

This morning, Captain Mirabelle unrolls an old yellow parchment that crackles beneath her paws. “Shipmates! Here is the map to Treasure Island. Who knows how to read a map?”
Filou has never read a map in his life. But his paw shoots straight up, faster than his brain. “Me! Easy!”
High above, perched in the crow's nest, Sardine, the little mouse lookout, wrinkles her nose. Sardine is sharp: Filou is holding the map upside down.

“Hard to port!” Filou orders. The Seashell swerves to the left… and heads straight for rocks as sharp as teeth. Screeech! The hull scrapes past, just nicking by.
“That was… just to test the helm,” stammers Filou, looking rather sheepish. “Hard to starboard!”
To the right bobs a swarm of pink jellyfish, quite surprised to see a ship sail by. “Pardon me, ladies,” whispers Filou over the side.

Far behind, a black ship glides through their wake, without a sound. It is Clawford, the pirate cat. Spyglass to his eye, he dreams of having the treasure all to himself.
“That fox has the map,” he sneers. “All I have to do is follow him!” So, when Filou turns left, Clawford turns left. And when Filou sails in circles…
… Clawford sails in circles too. Three whole times. “Where on earth is that silly fox going?” he grumbles, clutching his head in his paws. He is starting to feel very dizzy indeed!

The sun dips down, turning the sky bright orange. Not a single island on the horizon. The crew is hungry, the captain yawns, and in Filou's tummy, something twists tight like a sailor's knot.
No matter how hard he stares at the map, scratching his head… Lines, crosses, little squiggles: he cannot make head or tail of it. Not a single thing.
“If I tell them,” Filou thinks, curled up on the deck, “they will know I cannot do everything. And then… will they still like me?”

Night has fallen. A tiny voice tickles his ear: it is Sardine, perched upon his shoulder. “Filou… do you want to know a secret? I cannot swim.”
“You? A pirate?” Filou gasps. “Oh yes! So the captain teaches me, a little bit every day.” And Sardine paddles her little paws in the air. Filou gulps, then whispers softly:
“Sardine… I cannot read maps.” And just like that, something wonderful happens: the knot in his tummy unravels. It does not feel tight at all anymore.

In the morning, Sardine turns the map the right way up. “Look. The dotted line is our path. And the big red cross…” “…is the treasure!” cries Filou. He learns very quickly, once he is ready to try.
Standing before the whole crew, Filou clears his throat, a paw upon his heart. “Captain… I made a mistake. I didn't know how to read the map. But now, I know a little bit.”
Captain Mirabelle bursts into a hearty laugh that makes her whiskers twitch. “Shiver me timbers! That takes a lot of courage to admit, shipmate. Come on, take the helm!”

This time, The Seashell sails straight ahead, her sails billowing. An island! A palm tree! And beneath the palm tree, a chest brimming with gold coins that shine like little suns.
And Clawford? He is still sailing in circles, out there in the middle of the sea, his chin held high. Never, ever in his life had he dared to ask anyone for directions.
That evening, Filou falls asleep on deck, curled into a ball, the map tucked safely under his paw. The right way up. Tomorrow, Sardine is going to teach him the names of the stars. And he is going to teach her how to swim.


=== filou_dino
titre: Filou and the Speckled Egg

In Fern Valley, where the dinosaurs live, Filou trots along with his nose to the ground. He sniffs, he snuffles: he is looking for something extraordinary. Just like every morning.
And that morning, between two giant ferns, he finds it. An egg. Huge. All speckled with blue. Even standing on tiptoe, Filou is smaller than the egg!
“Goodness me! The most beautiful egg in the world!” cheers Filou, resting his cheek against the shell. “I found it, so… it belongs to me.”

Filou pushes the egg, heave! He rolls it, hup! Bracing his little paws, he rolls it up the bank to his hiding place. A treasure is heavy work.
His friend Tric, the little triceratops, sniffs the shell and tilts his head. “Whose egg is this?” “Mine!” Filou replies, his nose in the air. “I found it.” “Yes, but… before you found it?”
Filou pretends not to hear. Quickly, he tucks the egg into large leaves, all snug and warm, then sits in front of it, as straight as a guard.

Suddenly… tap. Tap tap. Craaack! A crack runs across the shell. Then two. And out pops a little blue head, with two big, shiny eyes.
The baby looks at Filou. Filou looks at the baby. “Mmmma?” babbles the little one. “Oh no, no, no!” Filou protests, waving his paws. “I'm Filou.”
“Mmma!” repeats the baby, delighted. And he snuggles up against Filou with all his weight, just like a big, warm cushion.

A baby dinosaur is hungry. All the time. Filou runs to gather an armful of leaves. More leaves. And even more leaves! His paws are completely worn out.
A baby dinosaur follows you everywhere. Into the river, splash! Into the brambles, ouch! And right onto Filou’s back, oof!
But when the little one dozes off, his snout nestled in Filou’s neck, letting out tiny snores… Filou thinks he has never had anything quite so precious.

That night, a song rises through the valley. A long, deep song, so sad that even the stars seem to be listening. “Moooo-oo… Moooo-oo…”
The baby lifts his head, craning his neck. His eyes shine. “Mmma?” But this time, he is not looking at Filou. He is looking far, far away.
Filou knows very well who is singing like that. It is a mummy searching. He shuts his eyes and covers his ears with both paws, as hard as he can. It does not work.

“If I take him back, I won’t have anything left,” whispers Filou. “No more treasure. No more warm cushion.” Tric does not answer. He just comes and sits right beside him.
The song starts again, carried by the wind. “Moooo-oo…” And Filou thinks of his own mummy. What she would do, if one evening he was no longer in his burrow.
So he gets up. He has a big lump in his throat, but he gets up. “Come on, big boy. Let’s take you home.”

They trudge along all night, bumpety-bump. At sunrise, an immense shadow falls over Filou: a neck as long as a tree, and right at the top, two eyes full of tears.
“Mmma!” cries the baby, and he runs, he stumbles, he runs again. The mummy sniffs him, licks him, and cuddles him close. The whole valley trembles with joy.
Then the enormous head comes down, down, all the way to Filou. And the mummy gently blows on his nose, ruffling his fur. In dinosaur talk, that means thank you.

Filou heads back with empty paws. No egg. No treasure. And yet, he skips down the path, light as a feather, as if he had sprouted wings.
Ever since, every morning, a little blue dinosaur waits for him by the river. Little? Not so little anymore: he has grown ever so much! But he still says, “Mmma!” and Filou does not correct him anymore.
And on some days, when the mummy agrees, Filou climbs right to the top of her long neck. From up there, the whole valley stretches out beneath his paws. It really is something extraordinary.


=== filou_chevaliers
titre: Filou and the Dragon of the North Tower

At Highrock Castle, everyone admires Sir Gontran, the knight peacock. His armour sparkles, his tail fans out grandly, and would you believe it, he once saw a dragon. From far away. One day.
“Pffft,” scoffs Filou, leaning against his barrel. “A dragon? Well, I’ve seen one up close!” Heads turn. It feels rather nice, all those heads turning.
“And what’s more... I chased it away! With just a stick!” he boasts. It isn't true. But it popped out all by itself, just like a hiccup.

Next day at the fountain, everyone asks: “Was your dragon big?” Filou stretches his arms out as wide as he can. “As big as... a house!”
The day after that, at the market: “And did it breathe fire?” Filou puffs out his cheeks, claws in the air. “Fire, smoke, and lightning bolts out of its ears!”
From ear to ear, Filou’s lie grows and grows, like a snowball rolling down a hill. And a snowball never stops on its own.

One morning, trumpets blare. Queen Hermine sends for Filou. “Brave fox! A dragon has moved into the North Tower. It smokes, it rumbles, and all my jam smells of soot!”
“You, who chase dragons with a stick, go and save us!” The whole court claps. Sir Gontran flaps his wings too, with a funny little smirk.
Filou opens his mouth to tell the truth. But there are so many people, so many eyes watching him... “E-easy peasy,” he stammers. And his knees go knock-knock-knock.

The staircase in the North Tower winds round and round, with a hundred steps. At every step, Filou thinks: “Turn back!” And at every step, he thinks: “What will they say?”
Up at the top, something rumbles. GRRR-RUMBLE! A wisp of smoke drifts under the door. Filou grips his stick, his fur standing on end. Never in his life has he been so scared.
With the tip of his paw, he pushes the door open. Creeeeak... And behind the door, there is...

... a tiny purple dragon girl, sitting on the floor, weeping big round tears. “Hic!” A little flame pops out of her nose. “Hic!” The curtain catches fire. Quickly, she pats it out with her tail!
“Please don’t chase me away!” she begs. “My name is Ember. I’ve had the hiccups since Tuesday. Hic! I burn everything I touch, so nobody wants me anymore.”
Filou sets his stick on the floor. This isn't a monster as big as a house. It’s someone very small who is feeling very sad, with the hiccups on top of it all.

“I know a cure for hiccups,” Filou announces. He leaps up to startle her: “BOO!” “Hic!” No luck. He makes her drink upside down. “Hic!” Still no luck.
So Filou sits against the wall, his ears drooping, and shares a secret he has never told anyone. “You know, Ember... I’ve never chased a dragon. I made it all up. I just wanted people to look at me.”
Ember’s eyes go wide and round. She is so utterly amazed... that she forgets all about her hiccups. No more hics. No more flames. Just a warm little giggle.

Now comes the hardest part: walking back down the hundred steps, with Ember’s paw in his. In the courtyard, the whole castle is waiting for their hero.
Filou takes a deep breath. “I didn't chase the dragon away. I’ve never chased one at all. I lied, and my lie grew bigger than me. This is Ember. She only had the hiccups.”
A long silence. Sir Gontran drops a feather in shock. Then Queen Hermine steps forward and rests her paw on Filou’s head. “It takes more courage to say that, little fox, than to climb up a tower.”

From that day on, Ember lives at the castle. She lights the fireplaces, toasts the bread with a gentle puff, and warms the Queen’s bath. Without burning a thing. Well, almost!
And Filou? Perched on the fountain or on a barrel at the market, he still tells stories. Stories of dragons as big as houses!
But now, he always starts with: “Once upon a time...” That way, everyone knows it's make-believe. And everyone still listens, wide-eyed and amazed.


=== filou_espace
titre: Filou and the Joke That Went Too Far

Aboard the rocket ship The Kettle travel two astronauts. Filou, who loves telling jokes. And Bip, the little round robot, who does not understand jokes at all.
When Filou cries out, “It’s raining cats and dogs!”, Bip presses his big blue eye against the porthole. “Error. No cats detected. No dogs detected.” And Filou rolls on the floor, laughing until his tummy hurts.
Bip tilts his head. Why is that funny? It is a mystery. But when Filou laughs, his little antenna starts blinking pink. And he quite likes that.

So Filou plays tricks on Bip. He hangs a stripy sock on his antenna. Another day, he makes him believe the Moon is made of cheese. Bip brings back a little piece, just to taste it.
Bip never gets cross. He simply says, in his quiet little voice, “I do not understand. But you are laughing. So it is good.”
Only here is the thing: after a while, Filou’s jokes become a little less funny. And a little bit prickly, just like nettles.

One day, the rocket stops at Three Moons Station. There are astronauts from all over the galaxy. Filou wriggles with excitement: he wants to make them all laugh, all at once.
He climbs onto a crate. “Look at my robot! He is so silly he searched for a button to turn off the Sun! Right, Bip, you old tin can?”
Everyone laughs. Everyone except Bip. His antenna does not blink pink. It droops down and turns completely grey. “Old tin can. Understood,” he whispers. This time, he understood.

The next morning, Bip’s seat is empty. No “Good morning Filou, it is zero seven hundred hours.” No beep. Nothing.
“Oh, he’s just sulking,” grumbles Filou. “He’ll be back.” Then he tells a joke out loud, just to see. Nobody replies, “I do not understand.” The joke falls flat on the floor, all by itself.
It is funny how big and quiet a rocket ship becomes when you are all alone inside.

Filou pulls on his spacesuit and dashes out among the moons. “Beeeep!” On the first moon: nobody. On the second: nobody. A great big emptiness grows in his tummy, just like in outer space.
With his paw on his heart, he thinks back to the dull grey antenna. And to the laughter, too. He was not laughing with Bip. He was laughing at Bip. And that is not the same thing at all.
On the third moon, right at the edge of a crater, he finally spots a tiny yellow dot, sitting still. A light that is not blinking any more.

Bip is sitting in the middle of a heap of dented scrap metal. “This is where I belong,” he crackles softly. “This is the corner for old tin cans.”
Filou wants to do a cartwheel, pull a silly face, do a clown trick to fix everything. But he knows deep down that this is not what is needed. So he sits down quietly in the moon dust.
“Bip. What I said was mean. I hurt your feelings just to make the others laugh. I am sorry. You are not an old tin can. You are my co-pilot. And my friend.”

Bip stays silent for a long moment. His little cogs go tick, tick, tick. “Analysis in progress… The word sorry has been detected. It feels… warm. In here.” And he points to his metal tummy.
“Will you still tell jokes?” asks Bip. “Yes,” promises Filou, holding up a paw. “But jokes where we both laugh. Will you tell me if it stings?” “I will tell you.”
And ever so gently, at the tip of his antenna, a little light flickers back on. Not grey. Pink.

That evening, inside the rocket, Filou finds a stripy sock on his left ear. He looks at Bip. Bip looks up at the ceiling. “Error. No sock detected,” says the robot, looking completely innocent.
Filou bursts out laughing. Bip starts to wobble and makes a funny noise, like a singing kettle: “Hee. Hee. Hee.” It is his very first laugh.
And The Kettle sets off again, whistling among the stars, with two astronauts on board. A fox who tells jokes. And a robot who is beginning to understand them.


=== filou_foret
titre: Filou and Margot's Nest

In Four Oaks Forest, Filou has a treasure: a golden marble. When he rolls it in the sunshine, it twinkles like a little star fallen in the grass.
But one morning... whoosh! A black-and-white shadow dives from the sky. One snap of a beak, and the marble has flown away.
“Chack-chack-chack!” cackles a voice, high up in the branches. It is Margot the magpie. Anything that shines, she pinches. And anything she pinches, she never gives back.

Filou stamps his paws, furious. Red right to the tip of his tail, which is not easy for a fox who is already ginger! “She’ll see about that, the little thief!”
With the tip of a stick, he draws a plan in the dirt. A rope. A basket. A bucket of mud. And to lure her in: a lovely shiny spoon. “She lands, the basket drops, and splat! Easy-peasy!”
“And then what?” asks Hazel the squirrel, from the top of her stump. “Then, she’ll be covered in mud from beak to tail, and everyone will laugh at her!” Hazel does not reply. She just scratches her ear.

To set his trap properly, he needs to know where Margot nests. When evening comes, Filou creeps up the tallest pine tree in the forest on velvet paws, without snapping a single twig.
Way up high sits an enormous nest. And in that nest... buttons, keys, spoons, a whole jumble of sparkling treasures! It looks just like a pirate’s cave.
Right in the middle, resting on a cushion of moss: his golden marble. Filou reaches out a paw... and freezes, ears pricked. Margot is talking. But to whom?

“Good evening, Mrs Spoon. Have you had a lovely day? And what about you, Mr Button? Oh, you never answer, do you...”
Margot arranges her treasures in a ring, like guests around a table. Ever so gently, she pours them drops of dew in walnut shells. Nobody drinks.
“Good night, everyone,” whispers Margot. She curls up, tucking her head under her wing, all alone among her treasures. And Filou hears a tiny little sigh, without the slightest cackle.

Filou climbs down without his marble. At the foot of the pine tree, his trap is waiting beneath the moon: the rope, the basket, the bucket of mud. He gazes at it for a long time.
Sitting in the grass, he thinks of the sparkling nest. And the guests who never answer. “She has everything,” he thinks. “And she has nobody.”
So Filou does a very strange thing. The bucket, he tips it over—splosh! The rope, he coils it up. And he only keeps the basket.

The next day, at the foot of the tall pine tree, a tablecloth is spread on the grass. On it are blueberries, a yummy hazelnut cake, and three walnut shells full of sweet syrup.
“Is it a trap?” asks Margot suspiciously, from her branch up above. “It used to be,” Filou admits. “Now, it’s a picnic. Coming?”
Margot hops from one foot to the other. Nobody has ever invited her anywhere. How do you even do it? She hops down one branch. Then another.

Margot nibbles the cake with the tip of her beak. Then she explains how, from high above, the forest looks just like a giant broccoli. Hazel giggles. Filou laughs. And Margot laughs too, “chack-chack”, but it does not sound unkind at all now.
With the last crumb swallowed, she flutters off without a word. Filou’s ears droop. But there she is already, flying back in a hurry, with something shiny in her beak.
Plink. The golden marble rolls across the tablecloth. “It doesn’t shine as bright as I thought,” Margot mutters, looking away. “It’s much nicer when someone answers.”

Ever since, every afternoon, they play marbles in the clearing. Margot cheats a little. Filou does too. Hazel counts the points on her paws, very solemnly.
Way up high, at the top of the tall pine tree, there is still a glittering nest. But in the evening, it is often empty.
That is because Margot comes home late now, among the fireflies. She has friends to walk home.


=== filou_ocean
titre: Filou and the Almost-Finished Submarine

Filou had decided to explore the bottom of the ocean. And to do that, he was building himself a submarine on the beach. All by himself, in just one morning. “Easy-peasy!”
A barrel for the hull. A saucepan for the porthole. Three crooked nails here, a bit of string there. Bang, bang, bang! Chop-chop!
“You haven’t put any glue around the porthole,” pointed out a seagull. “No time!” Filou replied with a shrug. “It’ll do just fine.”

And splash! Down sank the submarine, nice and gently. Yellow fish, pink coral, dancing seaweed... “Look at that!” trumpeted Filou. “The greatest explorer of all time!”
Drip. A drop landed right on his nose. Filou wiped it away with his paw. Drip. Another one. “It’s nothing. It’ll do just fine.”
Drip, drip, plop. Soon he was paddling about with water up to his ankles. And the fish were watching him curiously from the other side of the saucepan.

Whooosh! The porthole came loose. Water gushed in, bubbling fiercely. “Help!” Filou plugged one hole with a paw, another with his tail, a third... but he didn't have enough paws!
Luckily, someone else had eight! Quick as a flash, eight orange arms wrapped around the barrel, plugging all eight leaks at once.
It was Octave, the bespectacled octopus, watchmaker of the reef. “Good day to you,” he greeted very politely. “Your boat is leaking. I don't think it was quite finished.”

Octave towed the submarine to his workshop inside a coral cave. In there, everything had its place: screws sorted by size, seashells by colour, and eight tiny screwdrivers, one for each arm.
“We shall fix it together,” Octave offered. “First, we sand it.” “Does that take long?” “Yes.” “And then?” “We glue it. And wait for it to dry.” “Wait?!” gasped Filou.
Filou sighed so hard that a string of bubbles popped out. But he took the sandpaper all the same. Scritch, scratch, scritch.

After five minutes, Filou couldn’t bear it any longer. While Octave’s back was turned, he slapped a plank on as fast as he could, neat and quick. “There! Finished!”
The plank held for one second. Two seconds. And clatter! It tumbled right onto his paw. “Ouch!” Filou hopped on one foot.
Octave didn’t laugh. He calmly picked up the plank. “When I was a little octopus, I wanted to be finished before I’d even begun, too. Look.”

He gently opened a seashell. Inside, a tiny clock was ticking away, with golden cogs as delicate as a prawn’s eyelashes. “This one, I had to start over eleven times.”
Filou pressed his eye close. It was the prettiest thing he had ever seen. “Eleven times...” he whispered. “And you didn't get cross?” “Oh, I did. Seven times out of eleven.”
Filou gazed at his battered submarine for a long moment. Then he picked up the sandpaper again. Scritch... scratch... Slower this time. And he poked the tip of his tongue out, concentrating with all his might.

They sanded. They glued. And they waited for it to dry, playing dominoes with seashells. Filou lost every single game, but that was because Octave could think eight moves ahead.
Next came the screws, which had to be tightened one by one. Filou lined them up and counted them: forty-two. He didn’t even know he could count that high!
And when the last screw was tightened, the submarine gleamed like a brand-new penny. It was round, smooth and sturdy. It looked like a real one. It was a real one!

This time, they dived together, deeper than Filou had ever dreamed. Down where the sea was midnight blue, down where the fish carried lanterns.
Filou listened out for any drips, his ears prickled. Nothing. Not a single drop. Only, far away, the song of a whale.
“Well?” Octave asked with a twinkle in his eye. “Will it do just fine?” Filou grinned, his nose pressed flat against the porthole. “No. It’s ever so much better than that.”


=== filou_noel
titre: Filou and the Broken Sleigh

Christmas is coming to Four Oaks Forest. The snow has wrapped everything up in white, and Filou, pencil in paw, is making his list longer and longer. It already snakes across three metres of floorboards.
“A bicycle. A drum. A sailing boat. A second bicycle, just in case. And the biggest box of chocolates in the whole wide world…” His eyes sparkle.
“And what are you asking for the others?” asks Hazel, with her hands on her hips. Filou shrugs his shoulders. “The others? They can write their own lists!”

On Christmas Eve, Filou doesn't sleep a wink. Tucked behind the fireplace, with his little torch in his paw, he keeps watch. He wants to see his presents arrive. All of them. First.
Midnight strikes. Suddenly, outside: cling, cling, cling… CRASH! Something tumbles into the snow. Filou pricks up his ears. Then a deep voice grumbles, “Fiddlesticks!”
Filou scampers to the window. In the clearing, a sleigh has nose-dived into a snowdrift, a great big pile of snow blown by the wind. Nearby, a very old polar bear in a red coat is rubbing his back.

“Father Christmas!” Filou is dumbfounded. The old bear looks exhausted, slumped against his sleigh. And his reindeer, good old Gustave, has twisted his leg on landing.
“What a muddle!” sighs Father Christmas, throwing up his paws. “The whole forest to deliver before dawn, and no one left to pull my sleigh.”
Filou eyes the sack, which is brimming over. Right at the top sits an enormous parcel with a red ribbon, with his name on it. All he has to do is reach out a paw, and scurry back to bed.

But Father Christmas’s whiskers are trembling. So Filou hears himself say, quite surprised, “I can pull it! Easy-peasy! I might be small, but I know all the shortcuts.”
He slips into the harness. Oof! It is as heavy as a wardrobe. The snow crunches, the sleigh jerks forward, then glides between the fir trees. Sitting at the back, old Gustave points the way with the tip of his hoof.
First house: Hazel’s, with its round door. Father Christmas hands a little parcel to Filou. “Off you go, little fox,” he whispers. “Not a sound.”

Filou sneaks inside on tiptoe. Hazel is fast asleep, curled up into a ball. He places the parcel right by her pillow. She smiles in her sleep, as if she knows.
Then a funny thing happens inside Filou’s chest. It is soft and warm, just like a sip of hot chocolate. “More!” he whispers.
At Nestor’s, a pot of honey tied with ribbon. At Rosie’s, a pair of skates. At Plume’s, a book as big as she is. And with every parcel left on a doorstep, the warm sip of hot chocolate returns.

Only one house is left: Loupiot’s den, right at the end of Pine Tree Lane. Father Christmas rummages in his sack. He rummages again. Under his white beard, he turns pale.
“There’s a parcel missing,” he murmurs. “Must have fallen out when we crashed… Poor little wolf. It’s his very first Christmas.”
At the bottom of the sack, only one present remains. The biggest one. The one with the red ribbon, with a label that reads: Filou.

Filou hugs the parcel tight. Just from the shape, he knows it’s the bicycle. The one he has been hoping for all year. Then his eyes drift towards the little earthen den, without a single garland.
He pulls out his pencil. With one stroke, he crosses out “Filou”. Then, sticking his tongue out, he writes: “Loupiot”. And he tiptoes inside with the parcel, quiet as a mouse.
When he comes back out, empty-pawed, Father Christmas does not say a word. He simply rests his big paw on the little fox’s head. And the day breaks, rosy pink across the snow.

On Christmas morning, under Filou’s tree, there is no bicycle. Just a tiny parcel. Inside is a golden sleigh bell, and a little note: “For my very best reindeer.”
Outside, happy cheers ring out. It’s Loupiot riding past on a red bicycle, wobbling from side to side because he can’t balance properly yet. “Filou! Look! Father Christmas remembered me!”
Filou ties the bell to his scarf and dashes behind the bicycle to steady it. Cling, cling, cling! Next year, his list will be much, much shorter. But there will be ever so many more names on it.


=== filou_halloween
titre: Filou's Three Costumes

It is Halloween night. In Four Oaks Forest, a glowing pumpkin grins on every doorstep. And behind every door, there are sweets. Filou is already licking his lips.
The rule is simple: you knock, you shout, “Trick or treat!”, and you get a handful of goodies. Just one handful per house.
“Just one?” chuckles Filou, hiding behind a bush. “That's hardly enough for a fox of my talent.” He has a plan. And three costumes at the bottom of his bag.

At old Mathurine's house, he knocks dressed as a ghost. “Woo-woo! Trick or treat!” One handful. He dashes behind a bush, changes his costume in the blink of an eye, and comes back as a witch.
“Hee-hee-hee! Trick or treat!” “Well now, another little witch,” smiles the tortoise, whose eyesight isn't what it used to be. A second handful! And here he is again, dressed as a pirate.
Three costumes, three handfuls, at every single house. “Easy peasy!” His bag swells and swells and swells. Soon it is so heavy that Filou has to drag it through the leaves with both paws, puffing and panting.

“Ooh! You've got a whole mountain of them!” gasps Rosie, her little bag in her paw. “Will you share with us?” asks Nestor. Filou hugs his treasure tight. “Certainly not. I earned them. Every single one.”
He settles onto a tree stump, well out of the way, far from the pumpkins, and gets down to business. One toffee. One lollipop. Two marshmallows. Another toffee.
The first ten are delicious. The next ten are nice. The ten after that... he chews them anyway.

Just then, a little ghost walks by, with big grey paws poking out from under the sheet. It's Loupiot, the wolf pup. His bag dangles from his paw, completely flat.
“Didn't you get anything?” “I always arrive just after someone else,” sighs Loupiot, turning his bag inside out. “They told me: sorry, a ghost, a witch, and a pirate took the lot. They must have been very hungry.”
Filou chokes on his toffee. The ghost, the witch, and the pirate... he knows all three of them very well indeed.

And right at that moment, his tummy rumbles. Grrr-gloop! Then again. Grrr-gloop-gloop! Filou turns green. A proper witchy green, without a speck of face paint.
“Ouch...” he groans, doubled over. All those sweets are clattering inside him like pebbles in a bucket. There's still a whole bag full, and he can't bear the sight of another one.
He looks at Loupiot's flat little bag. He looks at his own bulging sack. Even with a tummy ache, it doesn't take a genius to figure it out.

“Loupiot... the ghost, the witch, and the pirate, they were all me,” Filou confesses. “I took your share. And everyone else's.” Loupiot's eyes go wide beneath his sheet. “All three? You must run awfully fast!”
Filou pours sweets into the wolf pup's bag. One handful. Two. Ten. Until it's as plump as a pumpkin. His tummy still aches, but something else feels much better.
“Come on,” he says, heaving the bag onto his shoulder. “Other friends missed out too.”

They make their way around the clearing. A handful for Rosie, one for Nestor, one for Plume. And one for each of the little bunnies, who had only managed to collect hazelnuts.
As for old Mathurine, who had given away everything she had, Filou tiptoes up to leave his three finest toffees on her doormat. With a little note: “From a ghost, a witch, and a pirate. Sorry.”
When he is done, his bag is almost empty. Right at the bottom, one last lollipop remains. He looks at it. No, really, he doesn't even want it.

They sit in a circle around the biggest pumpkin. Sweets travel from paw to paw. “Taste this one!” “I'll swap you for a red one!” It is so much jollier than sitting all alone on the tree stump.
Filou sips a herbal tea that Plume made for him. Yuck. But Loupiot has curled up right next to him, describing every single sweet so he doesn't miss out on the fun.
The following year, Filou only brings one costume. A two-headed ghost: one fox head, one wolf head. Everyone gets their handful. And it is just the right amount.


=== filou_ski
titre: Filou and the Black Run

This winter, Filou is heading to the mountains, to the White Pines Resort. In all his life, he has never put on a pair of skis. “Sliding on snow? Easy-peasy!”
The instructor’s name is Mr Cabri. He is a wise old chamois with a silver whistle and heaps of patience. “Lesson one: the snowplough. Make a triangle with your skis. That is how you brake.”
“Brake?” Filou protests, stamping his feet. “I want to go fast!” “First we brake,” Mr Cabri replies calmly. “Then we turn. And only then do we go fast.”

On the beginners’ slope, tiny tots try their hardest to make the triangle, bottoms sticking out. Filou watches them and yawns. A slope for babies, that is what it is.
Way up high rises another slope. As steep as a playground slide. With a black sign stuck at the bottom. “That one,” Mr Cabri warns, “is for much, much later.”
Much later? Filou waits until the instructor has his back turned. And off he slips, sneaking towards the ski lift. “Snowploughs are for snails!” he sniggers.

At the top, the wind howls. The slope drops straight down under his skis. From up here, the resort is tiny, and the pine trees look like blades of grass. Filou gulps.
A marmot pokes her nose out of her burrow. “Do you know how to turn, little one?” “Of course.” “Do you know how to brake?” “E-easy-peasy.” The marmot pops back into her hole. She would rather not watch.
Filou pushes on his poles. Off he goes. Off he goes fast. Off he goes VERY fast.

“Wheeeeee!” For three seconds, it is wonderful. Then a pine tree rushes straight towards him. He needs to turn! Filou leans over. But the skis shoot straight ahead.
“Aaaargh!” The tree zooms past, missing his whiskers by a whisker. He needs to brake. Brake! How do you do it again? A triangle? A square? Filou had not listened to a word.
His skis get tangled. Filou takes off, somersaults three times through the air, and SPLAT! There he is, stuck in the snow right up to his ears.

When he pops his head out, the slope is gone. Nothing but snow, pine trees, and a fog rolling in, as thick as cotton wool. One of his skis has slid all the way down without him.
“Helloooo?” Nobody. He shivers, his paws freezing, snow down his collar. On just one ski, he tries to slide down, and flops flat on his face straight away.
Filou sits down, his ears drooping. For the very first time today, he does not say “easy”. Sheepishly, he whispers, “I should have learnt how to brake.”

Tweeeet! A blast from a whistle pierces the fog. Two horns appear, then a bright red jacket. It is Mr Cabri, with the lost ski tucked under his arm. The marmot had gone to fetch him.
Filou expects a telling-off. But Mr Cabri kneels down, brushes the snow off his ears, and clips his ski back on. “Were you scared?” “Yes.” “Good. You have to love the mountain, and fear it just a little.”
“Now, we go back down. Together. Doing the snowplough. You will see, it is the perfect time to learn.”

A triangle with the skis. Knees bent. Filou slides down at a snail’s pace behind the red jacket. But he slides down standing up. And when he wants to stop, he stops.
The next day, he is the first one on the beginners’ slope. Alongside the tiny tots, he practises making triangles. He tumbles, he gets back up, covered in powdery snow. And Pompon shows him how to bend his knees.
On Wednesday, he learns to turn left. On Thursday, to turn right. On Friday, he links ten turns without a single fall, and Mr Cabri blows his whistle with joy.

On the final day, Filou looks up at the black slope. It is still there, steep as a slide. “Next year?” he asks. “Or the year after,” smiles Mr Cabri. “It is not going anywhere.”
So Filou swooshes down the blue slope. One turn, two turns, the wind in his whiskers. He is going fast! But this time, he is the one in charge of his skis.
At the bottom, he sprays a shower of snow as he brakes. A perfect triangle. Perched on her rock, the marmot claps with both paws. “Now that,” she admits, “was well worth watching!”


=== filou_mer
titre: Filou and the Best Sandcastle

This summer, Filou is spending his holiday at Dunes Beach. And he has brought his treasure: a big red bucket, three spades, a rake, and some star-shaped moulds. He lines them up proudly on the sand.
He has a grand plan. The tallest, most beautiful sandcastle ever built. With four towers, a bridge, and a seagull feather for a flag.
“And I’ll do it all by myself,” he announces, puffing out his chest. “Easy-peasy! That way, everyone will know it’s mine.”

A little crab scuttles over sideways. “That’s a lovely bucket. Can I borrow a spade? You have three.” “No. I need them.” “All three?” “All three.”
Rosie arrives with Pompon. “Can we help? I know how to make very tall towers!” Filou spreads his arms in front of his building site. “Hands off! It’s MY castle. Go and play somewhere else.”
They wander off, looking glum. With his rake, Filou draws a big circle around his building site. “Keep out!”

All morning, he digs, pats, and tips out moulds. It takes a long time all alone. He has to gallop down to the water, rush back, and go again, bucket in paw. The sand dries faster than he can build.
A little further along, laughter rings out. Rosie, Pompon, and the little crab are shaping something using just their paws. It is all wonky. And they look like they’re having the time of their lives.
“Pffft. Their castle is ugly,” grumbles Filou, nose in the air. He has no time for laughing. A fourth tower is waiting for him.

At four o’clock, it’s finished. Four towers, a bridge, the flag. Pure splendour. Filou takes three steps back to admire it. “Come and look! Everyone, come and see my castle!”
His friends come over to look. “It’s very nice,” they say politely. Then they head back to play, without him. Filou stands there beside his masterpiece. How strange: he thought he would feel much happier.
And all the while, behind him, very gently, the tide is coming in.

A first wave comes and laps against the wall. Shllp! A chunk of sand collapses. “Hey!” Filou grabs a spade. He fills it in. A second wave. Shllp, shllp!
He runs left, he runs right. Quick, a ditch! Quick, a sea wall! But he only has two paws, and the sea has a thousand.
“Help! My castle!” The bridge tumbles into the foam. A tower wobbles. All by himself, he will never manage.

Rosie looks up. She could answer, “Sort it out yourself, it’s YOUR castle.” For one second, she thinks it very loudly. Then she dashes over. The others do too.
“The spades!” shrieks Filou. “Take the spades! The bucket! Anything you want!” The little crab grabs a spade in each claw. Pompon drags the rake, twice as big as he is.
They dig a giant moat, they build up a barrier. Rosie pats, the crab digs, Pompon carries water in his ears. The wave rolls in… and stops dead at the foot of the wall.

Saved! Well, almost. The bridge is missing, and so is a tower. Filou gazes at the damage. Then he looks at his three friends, covered in sand right up to their eyes.
“Would… would you help me rebuild it?” “So it’s not YOUR castle anymore, then?” teases Rosie, ever so casually. With the tip of his paw, Filou rubs out the big circle. “No. It’s ours.”
Rosie rebuilds the tower, even taller. The crab digs moats filled with real water. Pompon sticks shells everywhere, even where he shouldn’t. It is all topsy-turvy. It is so much better.

In the evening, the high tide sweeps it all away at last. The wall, the towers, the flag. Sitting side by side, they watch the castle melt into the water, just like a sugar lump.
Filou expects to feel sad. But no. The castle has gone, and yet he still has something left. Three friends, and plans to meet again tomorrow morning.
The next day, on Dunes Beach, a red bucket waits right in the middle of the sand. With three spades stuck inside, and a sign made of seaweed. On it, Filou has written: “Help yourselves.”
