=== nestor_pirates
titre: Nestor, the Pirate Who Always Said Yes

Aboard The Seashell sails a pirate who is two heads taller than all the other sailors. It is Nestor the bear cub. He has paws as wide as oars, and a heart as soft as a warm brioche bun.
Nestor has a favourite word, a round little word: “yes”. “Nestor, can you carry my barrel?” “Yes.” “Nestor, can you scrub the deck for me?” “Yes, yes.”
The truth is, Nestor fears one thing, and one thing only: that no one will like him anymore if he says no.

So, from morning till night, the deck echoes: “Nestor!” “Yes!” “Nestoooor!” “Yes, yes!” He carries, he polishes, he pulls, he heaves.
Even Sardine, the tiny little mouse, joins in. “Nestor, will you scratch my back? Right there. Higher up. Lower down.” He bends down patiently. “Yes, yes, yes…”
In the evening, his honey toast is still waiting on a barrel. He hasn’t even had time to watch the dolphins. Nestor nods off standing up, leaning on his broom.

One night, Captain Mirabelle hands him a big golden key. “Nestor, I am trusting you with the treasure hold. Open it for no one, do you hear me? I’m counting on you.”
“Yes, Captain!” That yes swells his chest with pride. It is a yes he chose himself.
He leans against the heavy door, the key around his neck. The moon rises, round and bright. In the hammocks, the crew snore in chorus.

Flip, flap… A shadow creeps up the hull and climbs over the railing. It is Clawford, the pirate cat, wearing his finest smile. A smile full of sharp teeth.
“Good evening, my big friend!” he purrs softly. “What a shame… all alone on guard duty, with nothing to nibble on. Here, I’ve brought you some honey. Friends share, don’t they?”
“Mmm… yes…” sighs Nestor, sniffing the pot. The honey smells delicious. And Clawford called him his friend.

“And between friends,” Clawford purrs, “we do little favours for each other. Why not open that door for me? Just to have a peek. You wouldn't say no to a friend, would you?”
The word “yes” is already rising in his throat, just like always. His paw grazes the key. Clawford rubs his paws together.
But deep down in his tummy, there is something else. A tight little knot that whispers this is wrong. Nestor closes his eyes and listens. It has a tiny voice, but it insists, and insists.

“N…,” Nestor begins. The word gets stuck. He isn’t used to it, that’s all. “Nnn…” Clawford leans in, grinning: “What was that?”
“NO!” It is a huge no. A bear’s no. A no that shakes the sails and sends Clawford’s bandana flying overboard.
“No. You are not my friend. A friend would not ask me to betray my captain. Take your honey, and scram!”

Clawford has never seen such a gigantic bear cub. He backs away, slips, tumbles into his rowing boat, and rows, rows so fast that he leaves one of his boots behind.
The crew rush out in their pyjamas, lanterns in hand. Feeling sheepish, Nestor looks at his feet. “I shouted… I said no… Are you cross with me?”
Captain Mirabelle rests a paw on his shoulder. “Cross?” she asks in surprise. “Cabin boy, your no is worth pure gold. In fact, thanks to you, we still have ours!”

The next morning, Nestor is nibbling his honey toast when it starts again: “Nestor, can you carry my barrel?” He takes a moment to think. “Mmm… Yes. That one, I don’t mind.” “Nestor, can you scrub the deck for me?” “No. But I’ll happily scrub it with you.”
And the most wonderful thing is, nobody likes him any less. Sardine even swears she likes him a little more. She has learned to scratch her own back, using a fork.
That evening, leaning against the railing, Nestor savours his honey toast right down to the very last crumb. And he watches the dolphins leap, for a long, lovely time, simply because he wants to.


=== nestor_dino
titre: Nestor and Grondar’s Roar

In Fern Valley, there is one rule everyone knows to the tips of their claws: when Grondar roars, you run!
Grondar is the biggest, greenest, scariest tyrannosaurus of all. When he roars, coconuts tumble from the palm trees. GRRROOOWWL!
And Nestor? Nestor is scared of thunderstorms, spiders, and his own shadow at bedtime. So Grondar… just hearing his name makes his knees wobble like jelly.

That day, the roar booms out, louder than ever. GRRROOOWWL! Tric dashes off in a cloud of dust. Birds take flight. Even Granny Dipla quickens her pace—and she hasn't run in a hundred years!
Nestor runs too, his cheeks bright red. But a little bear cub can’t gallop very fast. Especially with pockets full of biscuits.
He trips over a tree root, tumbles head over heels… and lands on his bottom in a clearing, his ears full of leaves. All alone. Well, not quite.

Right in front of him stand two huge legs, as thick as tree trunks. Above the legs, a giant belly. Above the belly, teeth. Lots and lots of teeth.
“GRRROOOWWL!” Grondar’s breath ruffles his ears and makes his scarf flap. Nestor squeezes his eyes shut, clutches his very last biscuit, and waits.
He waits… and waits… and waits. Nothing happens. So he peeks through one eye. Just one.

Grondar isn’t even looking at him. He is holding one giant foot in the air, not daring to put it down. And from his yellow eye trickles a tear, as big as a bucket. Splosh!
“Grrrowl…” groans the tyrannosaurus. Up close, it doesn’t sound like an angry roar at all. It sounds like an “ouch!”
Nestor crouches down to take a closer look. There, under Grondar’s foot, is a thorn. An enormous, sharp black thorn, dug in very deep.

Grondar tries to reach it. But oh dear, his arms are much too short! He wiggles, he twists, he grumbles… no luck. Nobody has ever helped him before. Everyone just runs away.
Nestor could sneak away. The path is right there, between the ferns. His legs whisper, “Run!” His heart thumps, thump-thump, thump-thump, like a drum.
But his eyes stay fixed on the giant tear. He takes a brave step forward. “Um…” he squeaks in a tiny voice. “Mr Grondar? May… may I take a look?”

Grondar lowers his enormous head, very close. Nestor can see his own reflection in every single tooth. His knees are shaking so much, he looks like he’s dancing!
He grips the thorn with both paws. “This might sting a little,” he warns gently. “I’ll count to three. One… two…”
“THREE!” He pulls with all his might. Pop! The thorn flies through the air. Nestor lands with a bump on his bottom. And Grondar…

… Grondar lets out a sigh so long that the ferns bend right over. Then he opens his jaws wide, and sluuuurp! One big lick from a rough, pink tongue. Now Nestor is soggy from his ears to his toes.
“Thank you,” rumbles a shy, deep voice. “It’s been hurting for three days. I roared so someone would come. But the louder I roared, the faster everyone ran.”
Nestor snaps his last biscuit in two. One half for him, one half for Grondar. For a tyrannosaurus, it’s barely a crumb. But he chews it ever so slowly, his eyes closed, making it last.

That evening in the valley, heavy footsteps make the ground shake. Thud. Thud. Everyone gets ready to run… then stops dead in their tracks. High up on Grondar’s head, a little bear cub is waving hello.
“Weren’t you scared?” gasps Tric, eyes wide with wonder. “Oh yes,” admits Nestor, holding up his paws. “I was scared the whole time! I shook from start to finish. Look, I’m still wobbling!”
He is still scared of thunderstorms, spiders, and his own shadow at bedtime. But now, when Grondar roars, Nestor doesn’t run. He pops a biscuit into his pocket, and goes to see what’s the matter.


=== nestor_chevaliers
titre: Nestor and the Too-Small Armour

At Highrock Castle, it is a big day: Queen Hermine is choosing her new knights. Nestor has dreamed of this forever. This morning, his heart is galloping.
But in the armoury, nothing fits. The helmet? It perches on just one ear. The breastplate? It goes crack! The iron boots? He can only squeeze in a single toe.
“You are too big,” sighs Pip, the little hedgehog squire, spreading his arms wide. “Too wide. Too… everything.”

Sir Gontran, the peacock knight, struts around showing off his feathers in his gleaming armour. “A knight, my big chap, is slender, light, and elegant,” he trills. “Just like me.”
Nestor tries to bow politely. Clatter-crash! His bottom knocks over twelve spears, three shields, and a bucket. The whole room bursts out laughing.
Poor little bear! Nestor blushes right beneath his fur and tucks his head into his shoulders. “Hmm… if only I were smaller,” he thinks. “If only I took up less space.”

So Nestor tries to shrink. He sucks in his tummy, tiptoes on the tips of his paws, and scrunches himself right at the back, onto the tiniest stool.
He only nibbles half a slice of toast. His voice drops to a tiny whisper. And when he squeezes through a doorway, he apologises: “Pardon me, door.”
But no matter how much you suck in your tummy, a bear cub is still a bear cub. And the little stool goes crack, too!

In the afternoon, the Queen’s golden carriage rolls onto the old drawbridge for the grand parade. The trumpets sound, the flags flutter in the wind… but the wooden boards creak, and the carriage is already tilting.
Suddenly… CRAAACK! A bridge beam snaps clean in two. The carriage tilts, tilts, right over the dark water of the moat. “Help!” cries the Queen, clutching her crown.
Sir Gontran rushes over. He tugs at the carriage with the tips of his wings. “Oof!” Nothing budges, not an inch. His armour might be sparkling, but it is no help at all.

The knights push, pull, and heave with all their might. All in vain! The carriage slips further. It is too heavy. Far too heavy.
Right at the back, Nestor unfolds to his full height among the splintered bits of stool. He forgets to suck in his tummy; he forgets to tiptoe.
Thud, thud, thud! The bridge shakes beneath his paws. “Out of the way!” he roars. And this time, he is not the one saying sorry.

Nestor slides right under the carriage. He plants his big paws flat on the boards. Then he braces his broad back—that back that was far too wide for every suit of armour in the kingdom.
“Hmmmmf!” He heaves. The carriage lifts by an inch. “HMMMMF!” By a whole paw. His arms tremble, but they hold fast, as sturdy as two oak trunks.
And very gently, without a single jolt, Nestor sets the carriage down on solid ground. Phew! The Queen has not spilled a single drop of her tea.

A hush falls over the courtyard. Then Queen Hermine steps down from the carriage, looking tiny before the great big bear cub.
“I wanted knights who were slender and light,” she declares. “I was wrong. What I needed was someone to lean on. Kneel, Nestor!”
Nestor kneels down. Even on his knees, he is a whole head taller than everyone else. And for once, he does not mind one bit.

They never did find a suit of armour to fit him. So the blacksmith made him one specially, using two cooking pots and a lid. It does not shine very much, but it fits him like a glove.
Sir Gontran never calls him “chubby” again. He mumbles, “Sir Nestor,” stiff as a board, looking the other way. And Pip has a favourite new hiding spot: right on the knight’s left shoulder.
Nestor does not suck in his tummy any more. He walks through doorways without apologising. And whenever he sits down, he is always given the biggest armchair of all. The one that never goes crack.


=== nestor_espace
titre: Nestor and the Light Eater

Nestor is an astronaut. It is a wonderful job, with just one tiny problem: in space, it is dark all the time.
Now, Nestor does not like the dark. In the dark, you never know what might be hiding in the corners. There could be anything. Or worse: anyone.
So, inside his rocket, he has hung up seven nightlights. A red one, a blue one, one shaped like a moon… Every evening, before going to sleep, he counts them with the tip of his paw, and feels safe and sound.

One evening, he counts: “One, two, three, four, five, six…” He counts again. Six! One hook is empty: the red nightlight has vanished.
The next day: five. The day after that: four. “Anomaly,” crackles Bip the robot. “Someone is eating our lights.”
And outside, through the porthole, things are stranger still. Over there, a star goes out. Poof! Then another. Poof! The dark is nibbling away at the sky.

Soon, only one nightlight is left: the little moon. Nestor snuggles under his blanket and hugs it tight against his heart. He cannot sleep a wink.
In the middle of the night… scritch, scratch. Something is tiptoeing through the rocket. Something round. With one single, shiny eye.
Nestor wants to scream, but his voice has run away to hide. The creature leans over the nightlight… opens a giant mouth… and… GLOOP!

Dark. Pitch-dark. The worst thing that could happen has happened! Nestor waits to feel very, very scared. But then, a funny little sound rises in the cabin. Is that… a sniffle?
In the dark, something glows softly. It is the creature's tummy: round, purple, and full of swallowed lights, just like a cuddly lantern.
“Please don't hurt me,” sniffs the creature. “My name is Gloop. It isn't my fault. I am too scared of the dark. So I eat lights, so I always have one inside me.”

Nestor cannot believe his ears. A monster… who is scared of the dark? “Well… so am I,” he admits. “I had seven nightlights.” “I know,” replies Gloop, looking sheepish. “They were delicious. I am sorry.”
From his pocket, Nestor pulls out a biscuit and snaps it in two. Snap, crunch! They munch in silence, side by side, in the soft glow of Gloop's tummy.
“Say, Gloop… If you swallow all the stars, it will be even darker. For everyone. And for you, too.” Gloop stops chewing. He had never thought of that.

They turn towards the porthole. Outside, the sky is almost empty. “I have an idea,” says Nestor. “It makes me a little bit scared. We are going to look at the dark. Together. Just for a tiny moment.”
He takes Gloop's paw. It is soft, and it is trembling. His is trembling, too. Then he gently lays the blanket over the glowing tummy, just like blowing out a candle.
At first, you cannot see anything at all. Then, little by little, their eyes adjust. And look… the dark is not empty. It is blue, a deep, deep blue, and as soft as velvet.

“You know,” whispers Nestor, “you can only see the stars when it is dark. In the daytime, they are still there, but nobody looks at them.”
Gloop thinks very hard. Then he puffs himself up like a big balloon, opens the porthole, and blows. Whoooosh! A star shoots out of his mouth. Then ten. Then a thousand!
The sky lights up again, twinkling brighter than before. And inside the rocket: a red nightlight, a blue one, a little moon… Bip counts them one by one. “Seven. All present and correct.”

That evening, Nestor turns on only one nightlight. The little moon. The other six he gives to Gloop, to brighten up his cave on the nearby asteroid.
“What if I am still scared?” asks Gloop, his arms full of nightlights. “Then you look out of your window. I will leave my moon shining. You will know that I am here.”
Ever since, whenever you travel through that corner of space, you can spot two little lights waving to each other in the night. And all around them: stars, stars, stars.


=== nestor_foret
titre: Nestor and the Little Wolf at the End of the Lane

In Four Oaks Forest, people say an awful lot of things. They say the river tickles. They say the moon is a hazelnut. And they whisper that at the end of Pine Tree Lane, there lives a wolf.
“A wolf with teeth this big!” says Hazel the squirrel, stretching out her arms. “And yellow eyes!” chatters Margot the magpie. No one has ever seen him. But everyone knows.
Nestor listens along, munching on his snack. “Hmm... And is he mean?” “Of course he is!” they cry together. “He’s a wolf!”

One day, Nestor is heading home from his granny’s house, with a basket of honey bread tucked under his arm. He takes a little detour to pick some blackberries. Then another. And blackberry by blackberry, there he is... on Pine Tree Lane.
Here, the trees huddle close together. It is dark and shadowy. Behind a mossy trunk, two grey ears poke out. Then two eyes. Yellow ones.
Nestor freezes. The basket trembles in his paw. The grey ears tremble too. In fact, they look even more scared than he is.

“You... you aren't going to shout, are you?” whispers a tiny voice. And from behind the pine tree steps a wolf pup. Tiny and scrawny, with paws far too big for him.
“Usually, they shout. Or they throw pine cones at me. My name is Loupiot.” His little nose twitches: it smells like honey bread. His tummy rumbles. Grrrouik!
Nestor looks at his teeth. They are small and sharp, it is true. But right now, they are only nibbling his lip, the way you do when you are trying not to cry.

So Nestor thinks back to all the stories. Then he looks at what is right in front of him. And what he sees is someone who is hungry.
“Hmm. Do you like honey bread?” “I don't know. I've never tasted it.” Nestor sits down on a tree stump and breaks his slice in two. The bigger half he hands to Loupiot.
The little wolf savours every bite with his eyes closed, honey all over his whiskers. “It’s like eating sunshine,” he sighs. And his tail wags so hard that the pine needles swirl up all around.

They chat all afternoon long. Loupiot knows heaps of clever things: how to tell which way the wind blows, how to walk without making a sound, and how to sing to the moon. “Awoooo!” “Hmmm-oooo?” tries Nestor. They burst out giggling.
“Why don't you ever come to the clearing?” Loupiot’s ears droop. “I went there once. Everyone ran away. So I just watch from far away. You always look like you're having such fun.”
Nestor feels a tug at his heart. He places a big paw on Loupiot’s shoulder. “Tomorrow, you're coming with me.”

The next day, when they arrive at the clearing, it is total chaos. “THE WOLF!” Hazel scampers right to the top of her tree. Margot flaps away into the sky. The rabbits dive into their burrows, leaving only two white tails sticking out.
Loupiot flattens his ears. “You see...” he whispers. He is already turning back, his tail tucked down. But a big paw stops him.
Nestor does not like speaking in front of everyone. His voice wobbles a little, but he stands up tall. “Have you ever seen him bite? No. Have you ever spoken to him? No, you haven't. Well, I have. His name is Loupiot. And he is my friend.”

A long silence. Then a pink nose peeks out of a burrow. It is Pompon, the littlest rabbit of all, the one who has not yet learned to be afraid.
He hops closer and closer. Loupiot flattens himself in the grass so he doesn't look so big. Pompon touches the tip of his nose. “It's all wet!” And the baby bunny rolls onto his back, giggling happily.
Then Hazel climbs down one branch. Then two. Margot lands on the grass, not too close just yet. “Ahem,” she coughs. “Can he play tag, this wolf of yours?” “I can play WOLF!” replies Loupiot.

Never before had there been so much running and laughing in the clearing. Loupiot wins every single round, so they decide he must count to twenty instead of ten. He thinks that is very fair indeed.
That evening, there is honey bread for everyone, and Loupiot gets the very first piece. Nestor brought two whole baskets. He had planned ahead.
In Four Oaks Forest, people still say an awful lot of things. But now, when an “Awoooo!” echoes from the end of the lane, everyone says: “Listen! That's Loupiot calling us to play.”


=== nestor_ocean
titre: Nestor and Ondine's Song

All summer long, Nestor had a friend unlike any other: Ondine. She was as big as an island, as blue as the evening sky, and whenever she laughed, she made great big waves.
Every morning, Nestor rowed out to sea in his little wooden boat. He tapped three times on the water: splash, splash, splash! And Ondine rose up from the deep, slow and dripping wet.
She carried him on her back and blew fountains high in the air. And in the evening, as the first stars twinkled, she sang: a slow, deep song that rocked the whole sea to sleep.

But one morning, the air smelled different. High up in the sky, birds were flying past, all heading the very same way. And the water felt colder against his paws than yesterday.
Ondine gazed at the horizon. “Little bear,” she whispered, “it is almost time. When the water turns cold, the whales must travel south. Far, far away. It has always been this way.”
“Leave?” stammered Nestor. “B-but… are you coming back tonight?” “I shall return in the spring.”

Spring! That was at least a thousand sleeps away. A big, tight lump rose in Nestor's throat. And because he did not know what to do with it, the lump turned into a giant sulk.
“Then you're not my real friend!” he grumbled, folding his arms. “Real friends stay!” And he rowed back to the beach without looking behind him.
That evening, Ondine sang all the same. Nestor buried his head under his pillow. But he could hear every single note.

The next day, Nestor had a plan. Down to the shore, he dragged the longest rope in the whole world. He tied one end to Ondine's tail, and the other to a big rock on the beach.
“There. Now you have to stay,” he declared, putting his hands on his hips. Ondine did not pull on the rope. She could have snapped it in an instant. Instead, she sighed, and her breath made a soft little cloud.
“Little bear. If I stay, the sea will freeze all around me. And far away, in the warm waters, a baby whale is waiting to be born. My baby.”

Nestor looked at the rope. He looked at his friend. And he looked at the sea, far, far away, where the water was warm.
Slowly, his paws untied the knot. It was the hardest knot he had ever undone, even though it wasn't tight at all.
“I don't want you to go,” he sniffled, pressing his cheek against his great big friend. And this time he cried for real, without any sulking. Ondine let him cry. She stayed right beside him, until the very last tear.

“I shall share a whale secret with you,” she whispered. “My song travels through the water. Far, far away. Farther than any boat can sail. If you listen carefully, you will hear me.”
“Hmm… What about me? Will you hear me?” “If you sing.” So, all through the afternoon, Ondine taught him her song. Mmmm-oooo… Nestor sang terribly out of tune, so out of tune that the fish pulled funny faces. Ondine thought it was perfect.
He gave her his blue scarf, tying it gently to the tip of her flipper. In return, she gave him a round, smooth seashell. “For whenever you think of me.”

In the morning, all the whales gathered out at sea. It was time. Ondine blew one last fountain, the highest of them all, with a bright rainbow dancing inside.
Standing on tiptoe, Nestor waved his paw until the great blue tail was just a tiny speck. Then nothing. The sea was as flat as a mirror.
His heart felt heavy, clutching the seashell close to his chest. But it was no longer a hard lump. It felt soft and warm, just like a cosy blanket.

The winter was long. Bundled up in his woolly hat, Nestor gathered shells to show Ondine. He practised his singing. Every night, he counted the sleeps, until he stopped counting at all.
On some evenings, he walked down to the jetty and pressed his ear right against the water. And there, from far, far away, deep down below… Mmmm-oooo. Two voices. One deep and low. And one tiny, brand-new voice.
Then, standing at the very end of the jetty, Nestor sang his answer back, dreadfully out of tune and wonderfully loud. Then he went off to bed with a smile. Spring always arrives in the end.


=== nestor_noel
titre: Nestor and the Christmas He Couldn't Miss

In winter, bears sleep. That is just how it is. As soon as the first snowflakes fall, Nestor's eyelids grow heavy, so very heavy, an enormous yawn escapes him, and off he goes, snoring right through until spring.
That is why Nestor has never seen Christmas. Not once! Every year, waving their paws, his friends tell him all about it: the twinkling tree, the candles, the carols, and the spicy cinnamon cake.
“This year,” Nestor announces, raising a paw, “I will not sleep. I cross my heart and swear on my honey pot. I want to see Christmas too!”

To stay awake, he tries everything. A handful of snow down his neck: brrr, it makes him shiver! He counts the snowflakes, he sings at the top of his voice. And yet he still drifts off, standing straight up, right in the middle of the chorus.
Rosie tugs his paw to gallop him round the clearing. Filou tells him jokes. Plume asks a thousand questions. “Nestor? Nestor!” “Eh? I wasn’t sleeping,” he mumbles. “I was just thinking with my eyes closed.”
Only three days left until Christmas. Nestor is hanging on tight. His eyes are as red as redcurrants, he ties his scarf back to front, but he balls his paws into fists and stays awake.

He helps carry the big Christmas tree. Up on his tiptoes, he hangs golden pinecones at the very top, way up high where nobody else can reach.
He tastes the cinnamon cake batter. Once, twice, three times, just to be sure. “So that’s what Christmas smells like,” he marvels, his snout completely smeared. Then he lets out an enormous, jaw-cracking yawn.
On Christmas Eve, he sits down for a minute by the crackling fire. Just one little minute. Just long enough to warm his paws.

“Zzz-snore…” His friends shake him like an apple tree. Rosie tugs his ear. Filou blasts on his trumpet. Plume tickles his nose with the tip of a feather. No use at all! A sleeping bear is like a mountain.
“He’s going to miss everything,” Rosie groans. “He’ll be so upset!” The three of them gaze at their big friend, smiling in his dreams, his snout still dusted with cinnamon.
“Well then,” Plume whispers, lifting a wing, “if Nestor can’t come to Christmas…”

Together, the three of them heave him onto a sledge. Oof! They bump and slide him all the way to his den, then tuck him up under three cosy blankets. Nestor doesn't even twitch an ear.
Then Christmas arrives. The tree twinkles, the candles dance, carols drift into the night. It is lovely. But at the table, there is one big empty chair, and everyone keeps peeking at it out of the corner of their eye.
After the cake, Filou jumps up. “I’ve got an idea.” Rosie leaps up too. “I’ve got the very same one!” As for Plume, she is already tying her scarf.

That night, on tiptoe, they creep into the den. Beside the bed, they set up a little tree. Right at the top, they hang a star. And under his pillow, they hide three parcels.
And on a plate, they leave the biggest slice of cinnamon cake. Wrapped up snugly in a tea towel, so it keeps just right.
In his sleep, Nestor twitches the tip of his nose. “Mmm… Christmas…” he mutters. Then off he goes back to sleep, while the snow falls softly and silently over the forest.

One morning, a warm sunbeam tickles his ear. Birds are chirping. The snow has melted away! Nestor peeks open one eye… and leaps to his feet! “CHRISTMAS! I missed Christmas!”
A big lump rises in his throat. But then he spots the little tree. The star. The three parcels. And the slice of cake, a little dry now, but still smelling sweetly of cinnamon.
In the first parcel, a striped scarf. In the second, a drawing of the four of them. In the third, an enormous alarm clock, with a note scribbled by Filou: “For next year.”

Nestor steps out of his den, his heart thumping. In the clearing carpeted with primroses, his friends are waiting for him. They have put on their red hats again and relit all the candles.
“Merry Christmas, Nestor!” They sing all the carols, a tiny bit out of tune, under the blossoming trees, and Nestor taps along to the beat. It is the only Christmas in the whole wide world with butterflies fluttering by.
Nestor savours his slice of cake, his eyes closed, his friends snuggled close beside him. “Mmm… So that’s what Christmas is,” he whispers. “I thought it was just a day. But it’s really about friends.”


=== nestor_halloween
titre: Nestor and the Creaky House

On Halloween evening, an idea pops into Filou's head. “What if we go inside the crooked old house at the top of the hill? The creaky one. People say it's haunted!”
“I dare you!” says Rosie. “Why not,” Plume replies. Everyone turns to look at Nestor, dressed up as a pumpkin. His tummy ties itself in knots. “Um… yes, yes,” he stammers with a tiny smile. “Not scared at all.”
That is not true at all. Just looking at the house makes Nestor's throat feel tight. But nobody else seems to be frightened. So he stays quiet.

The door groans. Creeeeak! Inside, it is pitch-black, it smells of dust, and cobwebs hang from the ceiling, as big as bedsheets.
“Not scared at all,” Filou claims, his voice a bit too squeaky. “Not scared at all,” Rosie whispers, walking right beside him. “Not scared at all,” Nestor repeats. His knees are knocking like castanets.
They creep down the hallway. Every floorboard creaks. Creak. Creak. And upstairs, something answers back. Thud!

“It's just the wind,” Plume says reassuringly. Thud, thud. “It's a shutter,” Rosie whispers. Then a long moan drifts down the stairs. Woooooo…
“Shall we go up?” Filou asks, biting his lip. He does not look terribly keen. But it was his idea. And Rosie accepted the dare. You can never back down from a dare.
Nestor stares at the stairs, dark as a deep well. His heart thumps. His paws are freezing, and the lantern wobbles. His whole body is telling him one thing, and it definitely isn't “not scared at all.”

He puts one paw on the first step, then stops. His thoughts spin round and round: “If I say it, they will laugh at me. They will say I'm a baby.”
Then another thought pops into his head: “But if I don't say it, we'll have to go up. And I don't want to go up at all.”
So Nestor takes a deep breath, and he says, quite softly: “I'm scared. I'm too scared. I'm not going up. I'll wait for you outside.”

Complete silence. Nestor waits for them to laugh. With his snout down, he stares at his pumpkin feet.
“Phew,” whispers a little voice. “I'm scared too.” It is Rosie. Rosie! “I've been scared since the front door,” Plume admits. “And I've been scared since the bottom of the hill,” Filou mutters.
All four of them look at one another. Then they burst out laughing, a big, belly-aching laugh that sends the dust flying. It feels so good that the house already seems much less dark.

Woooooo… The moan starts again. But now that they are not pretending anymore, they listen more closely. “It sounds like… someone crying,” Plume notices.
At the top of the stairs, a tiny shadow appears, hanging upside down. It is a little bat, no bigger than a pine cone. “Are you leaving already?” she sniffs, wiping away a tear.
“My name is Vesper. I live here. I got everything ready, the sweets, the paper chains… but nobody ever comes up. Everyone is too scared of my house.”

Nestor looks up at the stairs. It is still dark up there. He is still a little bit scared. “If we go up together,” he suggests, “and I can come back down whenever I want… then I will.”
They climb the stairs in single file, paw in paw. The thud-thud was just Vesper setting up her table! Waiting up there for them are glowing candles, garlands of autumn leaves, and a big bowl full of sweets.
“You are my very first guests!” chirps Vesper, glowing with delight. It is the best Halloween tea party in the whole forest. Right inside the scariest house.

On the way back home, Filou nudges Nestor gently. “I'm so glad you said something. I would have gone upstairs with my teeth chattering, without telling a soul.”
“Nestor is the bravest of all!” Rosie declares, lifting his paw high. “He said the hardest thing.” Nestor blushes under his pumpkin costume.
Ever since that night, whenever something frightens them too much, the four friends have a secret password. They never say “not scared at all” anymore. Instead, they say: “A little bit. How about you?”


=== nestor_ski
titre: Nestor and the Great Sledge Race

Today, at White Pines Resort, it is the great sledge race! The first one down to the bottom wins a chocolate medal as big as a dinner plate.
Nestor dreams of winning it. Usually, he always comes dead last: too slow to run, too heavy to climb. But on a sledge, the heavier you are, the faster you go! He pats his big, round tummy.
“For once,” he thinks, gripping his rope, “I'm going to win!” Mr Cabri puts the whistle to his lips. Tweeeet!

Nestor’s sledge begins to glide forward. Then it picks up speed. Faster. Faster! He zooms past Plume. He zooms past Filou. He even zooms past Rosie, who can hardly believe her eyes!
“Wheeeee!” The wind pins his ears right back. The pine trees rush by in a blur. Nestor is in the lead. First place! Never in his whole life has this happened before.
Far below, he can already spot the finish line between two red flags, and the medal gleaming in the sun.

Just then, a tiny sound reaches his ears from the side of the track. A squeaky little cry. “Help…”
In a deep snowdrift, a sledge is overturned, runners sticking up in the air. And underneath, two little paws are wriggling. It’s Pip the hedgehog! He missed the bend.
Nestor is flying like the wind. In one second, he’ll have zoomed right past. Behind him, the others are catching up. “Someone else is sure to stop,” he tells himself.

But the others are still far behind. And Pip is so very little, stuck in that freezing hole. Nestor looks at the medal. He looks at the tiny paws.
Then he digs both heels deep into the snow. Skrrrrrtch! His sledge skids to a halt in a great white cloud. Rosie whooshes past. Then Filou. Then Plume.
“Oh, bother,” sighs Nestor. Then he trudges back up the slope, puffing and panting, knee-deep in the snow.

He lifts the little sledge. Underneath, Pip is as white as a snowman, teeth chattering. “I'm… I'm… so cold,” he shivers. “And I’ve lost my mitten.”
Nestor scoops him up from the snowy hole and rubs him warm. Then he tucks him inside his coat, snug against his big, cosy tummy. There is no better radiator than a bear cub!
“Did you stop your race for me?” asks Pip, wide-eyed. Nestor shrugs his shoulders. “Hmm… It wasn't that important.” That isn't quite true. But as soon as he says it, it feels true.

Pip’s sledge is smashed to pieces. So they both climb onto Nestor’s sledge together. Carrying one bear cub and one hedgehog, it glides peacefully down, in no hurry at all.
Tucked up warm, Pip gets his rosy cheeks back. Around every bend, he throws his arms in the air. “Again!” Nestor chuckles. Earlier on, he hadn’t noticed how lovely the mountain was, with all its sparkling pine trees.
When they finally reach the bottom, the race has been over for ages. Rosie is wearing the chocolate medal around her neck.

“In last place!” announces the loudspeaker. “Nestor!” A few animals giggle. But then Pip pokes his head out from the coat and tells everyone the whole story. The hole. The cold. The heels dug into the snow.
Nobody giggles anymore. Rosie looks down at her medal. She slips it off her neck. Snap! She breaks it into pieces. “It’s much too big for just me,” she announces.
Everyone gets a piece. And the very biggest chunk, she hands to Nestor, whose cheeks turn pink. “You were the real winner,” she says. “I know it, because you overtook me!”

That evening in the chalet, everyone is sipping hot chocolate. Pip has curled up into a ball and fallen fast asleep on Nestor’s tummy. Nobody dares to move him. Especially not Nestor.
On the mantelpiece sits a brand-new medal. It is made of cardboard, cut out a little bit crookedly, and coloured in with crayons. Pip made it himself. On it, he drew a bear cub with a big heart.
You cannot eat it. Yet Nestor presses it gently to his chest. He will keep it forever.


=== nestor_mer
titre: Nestor and the Yellow Rubber Ring

At Dunes Beach, all of Nestor's friends are in the water. Filou dives, Rosie floats on her back, and Plume splashes about in the shallows. But Nestor stays sitting on his towel, with his big yellow rubber ring around his tummy.
“Come on in, Nestor! It’s lovely!” “Mmm… in a bit. I’m digesting.” He ate three hours ago.
The truth is, Nestor is scared of the water. It never stops moving, it’s deep, and you can’t see what’s hiding underneath. Besides, bear cubs sink like stones. He is sure of it.

Even so, he creeps down to the water’s edge and dips a toe in. Brrr, that’s cold! A tiny ripple comes rushing in, no thicker than a pancake. Nestor jumps back ten whole paces.
“Just jump straight in!” Filou calls out. “Like me!” “Close your eyes and run!” adds Rosie. Nestor tries to run. But right at the water’s edge, his paws freeze all on their own, as if he has hit a wall.
He trudges back to sit down, his shoulders slouched. Out in the waves, his friends are laughing. Nestor feels very big, very heavy, and very alone on his towel.

“It scares you, doesn’t it?” It is Mr Otto, the lifeguard seal, wearing his bright red swimming cap. Nestor looks down at his paws. “A bit. A lot. The others aren't scared.”
“Shall I tell you a secret? When I was little, I refused to put my head under the water. A seal! My mother was so embarrassed.” “And then?” “Then I learned. One whisker at a time.”
“You don’t jump into the sea all at once. You get to know it first. Come along. Today, we only wet our feet. And that’s all.”

Only his feet? Nestor can manage that. Clinging tightly to Mr Otto’s flipper, he wades into the water up to his ankles. The tiny waves tickle his toes. He giggles.
“That’s enough for today,” the seal announces. “Already?” “Already. Tomorrow, up to our knees.” Nestor walks back to his towel with his chin held high. He has only wet his feet, but he feels as proud as if he had swum the whole ocean.
The next day: knees. The day after that: tummy. The yellow rubber ring bobs around him like a big duck.

On the fourth day, Mr Otto says, “Today, we blow bubbles.” Nestor dips his chin in. Then his mouth. Blblblblb! It bubbles away just like a kettle.
Suddenly, a wave washes right over his nose. He coughs, he splutters. His heart thumps fast. “I want to get out!” “Out we go,” Mr Otto replies calmly. “We’ll come back whenever you’re ready.”
Nestor sits on the sand, his nose prickling. He expects to be too frightened to ever go back. But after five minutes, he stands up all by himself, his brow furrowed. “Mmm… More bubbles.”

On the sixth day, Mr Otto teaches him the starfish float. “On your back, spread your paws, and let the sea carry you.” Nestor lies flat on the water, stiff as a board. “It’ll never hold me. I’m too heavy.”
“The sea carries whales, my boy.” Nestor lies back. The seal’s flippers gently hold him up. The water fills his ears. All he can see is the sky, bright blue, with a seagull gliding by.
“Shall I let go?” “… Yes.” The flippers slide away. And Nestor floats! All by himself. Like a big, furry starfish. Left behind on the sand, the yellow rubber ring watches on.

On the last day of the holidays, Mr Otto takes three steps back into the water. “Come over to me. Any way you like.” Nestor puffs out his chest. He kicks with his paws.
He paddles, he splashes, he swallows a mouthful of sea. It isn’t very graceful. But he moves forward. One stroke. Two. Five! He grabs hold of the seal’s flipper. “I swam!”
On the beach, his friends cheer with joy. “NESTOR! NESTOR!” Filou does a cartwheel. Rosie jumps up and down. “Five strokes! I counted them!”

That afternoon, Nestor splashes in the waves with the others. Not too far out. Only where he can touch the bottom. But right in the water. And when a rolling wave knocks him over, he pops back up laughing, with water in his ears.
On the sand, a tiny little rabbit gazes at the sea, clutching his towel tight. It is Pompon. Nestor waddles out of the water and sits down beside him, dripping wet.
“It scares you, doesn’t it? It scared me too, before. Here.” He slips the big yellow rubber ring around Pompon's tummy. “Today, we only wet our feet. And that’s all.”
